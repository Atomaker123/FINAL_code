Bohr's Model of an Atom

The Planetary Model

Danish physicist Niels Bohr proposed that electrons do not move randomly, but follow specific circular paths called "orbits" at set distances from the nucleus.

Postulates:

  • Electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed energy levels or shells.
  • Each orbit has a specific, constant energy.
  • Electrons can jump between orbits by absorbing or emitting energy.
  • Orbits closer to the nucleus have lower energy levels.

Bohr's model explained why atoms were stable and paved the way for modern quantum chemistry by connecting the structure of the atom to energy and light.
